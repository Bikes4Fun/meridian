What is Meridian?

A home system for people living with cognitive decline and the families who support them.

Meridian connects families, a web dashboard, mobile apps, and a kiosk so day-to-day care, location awareness, and emergency information stay in one system.

Meridian is built around a kiosk located in a conspicuaous location in the patients home. The Kiosk is a large-format touchscreen meant to stay in one place, stay visible, and stay on. The person at home uses it for time and schedule, family locations, messages, medications, and emergency information. The kiosk does not require a login; it is pre-authenticated to your Family Circle via the webapp or mobile app by the admin.*

Family members use the web dashboard (browser) to manage detailed settings, shared data, events, medications, ICE / POLST, permissions (where wired), and emergency alerts.

The mobile experience includes the native iOS app (TestFlight / in development) and a mobile-optimized web view inside the dashboard Mobile tab for check-in and messaging.

Overview

Family Circles

All data in Meridian belongs to a Family Circle — visible only to people you've invited.

A family circle is the group of people and settings Meridian uses for one household or care unit: shared calendar, medications, contacts, locations, and (where configured) the care recipient profile used for ICE / emergency.

  • Who uses it: Family members sign in to the web dashboard or mobile app with their account; the kiosk is usually logged in for the home or facility so the patient does not need to manage passwords.
  • What is shared: Events, medication lists, named places, check-ins, and emergency profile data are scoped to that circle — people outside the circle do not see them.
  • Care recipient: Many screens focus on one person receiving care (the care recipient). ICE / POLST and some health data attach to that person within the circle.

Each circle has one care recipient. Everyone else joins with a role controlling what they can see and change. The kiosk is pre-authenticated to the circle — the care recipient never signs in.

Exact roles and permissions (who may edit medications vs locations vs billing) depend on your deployment; ask your administrator if you are unsure what you can change.

Kiosk

Home screen

The default screen. Shows the time, date, upcoming events, and today's medications.

  • Clock — large-format current time
  • Date and time of day — morning / afternoon / evening, for orientation
  • Up Next — the next upcoming calendar event
  • Today's medications — doses for the current time window, each with a Mark taken button

Marking a dose taken refreshes the screen automatically.

Kiosk

Schedule screen

A full view of upcoming events from the shared family calendar.

Events are listed in chronological order. Managed from the web dashboard — changes appear on the kiosk the next time the screen loads.

Kiosk

Family screen

Shows where family members are, optional voice calls to contacts, and the "Where is everyone?" button.

Displays a map with each family member's most recent check-in. If the check-in falls within a named location, the kiosk shows the label (e.g. "Anne is at work downtown") instead of a pin.

Each check-in row can include a Call button when the server has a phone number for that person (same Twilio speakerphone flow as before). A Family voices section lists contacts with the same voice-call controls.

Tapping Where is everyone? sends a check-in request to family devices.

💡
The kiosk's own location can be named — so the care recipient sees "You are at your home on Maple Street" rather than a pin at their address.
Kiosk

Emergency screen

The care recipient's full emergency profile. Accessible any time via the red Emergency button in the footer.

Displays everything from the ICE profile maintained in the web dashboard:

  • Name, date of birth, DNR / code status
  • Conditions and allergies
  • Current medications
  • Emergency contacts (primary and secondary)
  • Medical proxy and power of attorney (name and phone)
  • Notes
  • Open document — if a POLST or DNR file has been uploaded

A Print button generates a formatted emergency PDF. The kiosk may auto-print when an alert is active, depending on configuration.

⚠️
Keep it current. Review medications and code status after any medical appointment or change in directives.

Supervised kiosk mode

When enabled (future), the person at home would see only what you consider safe—typically Home and Emergency—to reduce confusion or unsafe browsing at night. Unsupervised mode respects the per-screen toggles and time windows instead.

Per-screen hours

Optional windows (for example, hide the family map after 9 pm) are configured per screen. Times are intended to be evaluated on the kiosk in its local timezone.

Invites & temporary access

Pending invitations lists email invites that have not been accepted; you can resend or revoke them. Access expires on a member row is for short-term helpers (e.g. home health); access should end automatically on that date when implemented.

Exports, deletes, and two-person rule

Full family export and permanent delete are high-risk. The Settings layout includes options to require password re-entry and, optionally, a second primary caregiver to approve before an export is generated or data is destroyed.

Notification routing & quiet hours

Separate from roles for editing data: you can define which roles receive push or email for emergencies, medication digests, check-in requests, and (later) geofence events. Quiet hours delay routine messages; true emergencies may still break through per policy.

Reference

Named locations *

Give common places a plain-language label. When a check-in falls within the location's radius, the kiosk shows the label instead of a map pin.

Editors with permission to manage family settings (exact permission name TBD) can define named places with a label and radius so check-ins near that point show human-readable text on the kiosk.

Families choose how verbose labels are. Naming reduces confusion for someone who finds maps or coordinates disorienting. Where is everyone? is meant to reduce distrust of stale on-screen data by asking family to confirm locations as current. *

Location matching uses a haversine distance calculation. The nearest matching location wins when areas overlap. The default radius is 150 meters; each location's radius is configurable.

🕐
Editor in progress. Server-side matching is live. Once the editor ships, locations you define apply to new check-ins immediately. Until then, some setups require admin or database help. *

How families write labels

Labels can be brief or descriptive — the goal is that whoever reads it, including someone who may be disoriented, immediately understands where the person is and that it's somewhere expected.

  • "Anne is at work downtown"
  • "Evan is at High School in Colorado Springs"
  • "Dave's Office"
  • "You are at your home on Maple Street in Denver" — for the kiosk's own location

Data & privacy

What Meridian stores, who can see it, and what's protected.

Circle isolation

All data is scoped to your Family Circle. No other users or circles can access it. The kiosk is pre-authenticated — no credentials are ever exposed to the care recipient.

Location data

Location is generally submitted on an active check-in or request response, not passive background tracking. Location history is not stored by default; retention policies depend on deployment. *

Health & medical data

Medications, conditions, allergies, and ICE documents are stored within your circle only. Uploaded POLST/DNR documents can be deleted at any time from the ICE editor when supported.

Transport

All data is transmitted over TLS. Uploaded documents use access-controlled storage links. Encryption at rest and certifications may still be in progress. *

⚠️
Not yet production-hardened. Full encryption at rest, authentication hardening, and compliance certifications are in progress. Do not store real private or medical data unless your administrator has confirmed the deployment is ready.

Note on asterisks

A * on a heading or at the end of a phrase means one or more of:

  • not yet implemented,
  • preview or UI-only,
  • partial wiring, or
  • behavior that depends on server or configuration (for example APNs, HTTPS).

It is not a ship-date commitment. Unmarked sections describe expected current behavior when the feature is enabled for your deployment. If the live app disagrees with this guide, trust the product UI and your administrator.
